## Model and permissions

- D1. A Direct message (DM) is a Channel of kind `dm`, between exactly two distinct Members of the same Organization. Here “participant” means one of those two retained Member identities, independently of whether their access is currently usable. There is no group DM, name, visibility setting, Topic, Move to topic, add/remove participant or leave operation. Replies and Reply chains are allowed, always with `topic_id=null`. Existing public/private Channels have kind `channel`; their visibility and manager rules remain unchanged.
- D2. The pair is immutable and canonical: order its two Member UUIDs by their UUID value, and enforce unique `(organization_id, lower_member_id, higher_member_id)` in storage, not just by a lookup. Use Member ids, never Account ids or display names. Deactivation, reactivation, Role/name changes and grant renewal retain that pair and Channel id forever; no second Channel is created for it. Record an immutable DM `created_at` at creation for R4 pagination; renewal never changes it. Same-Organization composite references, distinct/ordered pair checks and grant constraints prevent a foreign or third participant, including direct SQL. Every new table has `organization_id NOT NULL` and the existing Organization context/RLS rules. Channels owns pair/grant data and SQL; Messages owns seq, Messages and read positions; Auth supplies Member identity/eligibility through its scoped interface.
- D3. Each participant has one explicit grant with its own durable `grant_id` and captured `membership_revision`. Access requires the actor to be active in that Organization, the request/socket's captured revision to equal their current revision, participation in this pair, and a grant for that revision. Every Role, including Owner/Admin, satisfies exactly that rule; there is no public access path or administrative bypass. A grant to a third Member must not confer access. The same deny-by-default `authorize` policy and Channels-owned visibility predicate govern list filtering, history, Reply chains, send/retry, mark-read, subscribe, replay, live delivery, #152 Mentions and #153 Search. SQL visibility and read-position triggers must agree with it.
- D4. Only an active non-Guest Member (Owner/Admin/Member) may explicitly open a DM, including an existing DM or renewal. Creating or renewing requires another active Member of the same Organization as peer, of any Role including Guest; resolving a pair the caller already has access to does not require peer eligibility (B2.1). A Guest cannot call open even for an existing pair; a Guest with a current participant grant may list, select, read, send and Reply, mark-read and subscribe exactly as another participant. Selecting an already accessible DM uses reads/subscription, not an open request that would deny a Guest. Knowledge of a Member id grants no access except the explicitly authorized open operation with that Member.
- D5. DM grants never carry a Channel manager right. All four existing Channel Capabilities (`can_add_members`, `can_remove_members`, `can_manage_managers`, `can_leave`) return `{allowed:false, reason:"dm_membership_fixed"}` for an accessible DM. Every Channel member/grant API, including GET, self-leave, stale-grant cleanup and no-op mutations, rejects a DM after access authorization. Exclude DMs explicitly from `RoleChangeGuard::lock`, private last-grant protection and every #311/#292 usable-manager count, SQL projection and Capabilities calculation. Demotion/deactivation cannot return `last_channel_manager` because of a DM; ordinary private-Channel protections and `last_owner` precedence remain intact. Role changes alone preserve usable DM grants, including a change to Guest.
- D6. The append-only migration preserves every existing Channel id, name, visibility, grant lifetime/right, seq, Message and Unread position. Backfill their kind as `channel`, not DM. Preserve Organization-wide non-DM name uniqueness across public/private visibility using the existing `lower(name COLLATE "C")` rule and existing name validation; DMs have no name and do not occupy that namespace. Constraints must not allow an ordinary Channel to evade name uniqueness or a DM to acquire ordinary Channel management/visibility through another API. The implementation adds only an append-only migration and regenerates the API contract.

## Open and renewal (B2)

- B2.1. With `renew` omitted/false, after caller authorization an existing pair with a usable caller grant returns 200 `Dm` **before peer eligibility**, with no grant/position change or publication. This includes an inactive peer or a lapsed peer grant. Otherwise open creates the pair, Channel, both grants and initial read positions atomically if absent, or renews **each lapsed grant of either participant** if the pair exists. These create/renew branches require an active same-Organization peer. To renew a peer's lapsed grant while the caller still has access, explicitly send `renew:true`; that branch checks peer eligibility and renews all lapsed grants, never replacing a current grant. Renewal still needs no current initiator grant: fresh active non-Guest Organization authority plus the checked active peer authorizes only this operation. No third-Member or inactive-peer renewal is allowed. Old captured request revisions never borrow a reactivated Member's new revision. This intent distinction was approved on 2026-11-14, amending B2 while preserving atomic renewal of all lapsed grants when renewal happens.
- B2.2. Each renewed grant captures that participant's current revision and gets a new `grant_id`, without a manager right. A still-current grant is unchanged: preserve its id, Unread position and subscription. For each newly granted/renewed participant, initialize Unread at the Channel's latest committed seq at renewal (0 if empty), using #142 access serialization and the shared #135 COMMIT barrier through access COMMIT. Earlier history remains readable but is neither Unread nor Mention unread. A send cannot commit between that baseline and the grant becoming visible. A duplicate open with two current grants is a reauthorized no-op; it never resets positions or replaces grant ids.
- B2.3. Serialize open/renewal with Organization changes: Organization NO KEY UPDATE -> existing DM Channel -> both Member rows in ascending id order -> grants in ascending Member id order -> discovery counters/index (P-D2) -> #142 Unread serialization/shared #135 for grant changes. For a new pair, serialize absence under the Organization lock before Members/new rows; the unique pair constraint remains the final defense. Re-read caller Role/activity/captured revision and pair/caller grant after the relevant locks, holding authority through COMMIT, including resolution-only no-ops. Only a create/renew branch enforces peer activity/current revision and renewal eligibility under those locks. If a preliminary lookup changes, restart before taking later locks; never discover an earlier-order lock after taking a later one.
- B2.4. Simultaneous A->B and B->A opens create one DM: one response is 201, the other 200 with the same id. Simultaneous renewals replace each lapsed lifetime once; the waiter observes current grants and does not reset Unread again. Open versus renewal has that same serialization, with no duplicate or partial pair. Open-first versus deactivation may commit its grants, then deactivation increments the affected revision and makes that grant stale. Deactivation-first denies a deactivated caller (`organization_unavailable`); for peer loss it denies create/renew (`member_not_found`) but an already-authorized caller's resolution-only open still returns 200 with `peer.active:false`. Reactivation concurrent with renewal must become visible and pass fresh authorization under the same order before renewal can succeed. Rollback/refusal changes neither participant's grant or position.
- B2.5. Unknown open outcome is recovered by refetching the authorized DM list (R4/E5), matching `peer.id`; omission from one page proves nothing, so explicitly follow `next_cursor` until found or exhausted if the id is unknown. This recovery traversal keeps its own immutable continuation under E6, independently of ordinary browsing and retained rows; match each accepted page before retention eviction. O5/list invalidations preserve its progress, and stale pages are re-requested from the same key under current authorization. A resolution-only retry with the same `member_id` returns the same Channel with 200 if its first call committed and the caller still has a usable grant, even if the peer was deactivated or their grant lapsed meanwhile. It returns current authorized fields, not the lost response's snapshot. Caller deactivation or an old captured revision gives 404 `organization_unavailable`; becoming Guest gives 403 `forbidden`, even with a usable grant. With a fresh active non-Guest caller but no usable grant, no pair is disclosed before peer eligibility: an inactive/unknown/foreign peer gives the same 404 `member_not_found` whether a pair exists or not; an active same-Organization peer permits the existing B2 create/renew operation (200 renewal, 201 creation). A retry with `renew:true` also rechecks peer eligibility and may return `member_not_found` after peer loss despite a prior COMMIT; refetch still recovers any DM accessible to the caller. Thus pair uniqueness is unconditional, but recovery success is conditional on current authority and intent; no client create id or blanket success-after-state-change guarantee is claimed. Definitive refusals are not automatically retried, and C6/C10 terminal Session/Organization loss stops recovery until explicit reauthorization.

## REST contract

All suffixes below start `/api/v1/orgs/{org}`. Cookie/bearer Account Session authentication and Organization authority are unchanged. UUID ids refer to Members or Channels as labeled. No request supplies an Organization, caller id, revision, grant id, name or visibility. Request objects reject unknown fields. Every response, including errors, uses `Cache-Control: no-store`. New open bodies use the existing 16 KiB limit; send keeps 32 KiB.

Target shapes (all fields required except `renew`; seq/count fields are nonnegative integers, UUIDs are strings):

```text
OpenDm = { member_id: UUID, renew?: boolean } // omitted = false
DmPeer = { id: UUID, name: string, active: boolean }
Dm = {
  id: UUID, kind: "dm", peer: DmPeer,
  capabilities: ChannelCapabilities,
  as_of_seq: integer, read_seq: integer,
  main_unread_count: integer, unread_count: integer,
  mention_unread_count: integer
}
DmList = { dms: Dm[], next_cursor: string | null }
ErrorBody = existing { error: string }
```

`id` is the underlying Channel id everywhere, including Message events and URLs. `peer` is the other participant's **current** Member display identity in this authorized snapshot, including retained name/id while inactive; no email, Account id, grants or revision is exposed. This is a DM-scoped identity lookup, not permission to list the peer in the Organization directory. `active` describes Membership activity, not the peer's grant or a promise that sending will succeed; the server always rechecks. `ChannelCapabilities` is the existing shape with D5's four denials. Ordinary Channel responses retain their existing fields and add `kind:"channel"`; DM responses omit `name` and `visibility`. Shared client representations discriminate by kind, never by a fabricated Channel name or `visibility:"private"`.

- R1. Each list page or held-id batch gets authorization, peer identity, committed `as_of_seq`, positions and all three counts from one read-only REPEATABLE READ snapshot, using batched owning-module queries, with no per-DM Member/Unread SQL. Filter authorized ids and select at most 50 before computing aggregates; a cursor lookahead may select one extra authorized id, never aggregate the whole list before limiting. Reads take no Member row locks. Empty authorized lists succeed, inaccessible entries are omitted with no placeholder/count/existence signal. An unavailable/stale caller returns `organization_unavailable`, not an empty success. POST returns the same fields from its transaction's committed state; it is not a response-time high-water promise and must not replace newer accepted baselines.
- R2. DM Unread uses P8 without Topic scopes: `main_unread_count = unread_count`, counting only other senders' Messages above `read_seq` and at/below `as_of_seq`. Sending changes no read position. #152 supplies `mention_unread_count` in this same shape/snapshot, with the same visibility, sender exclusion, grant baseline and read scope; it counts the unread Messages matching #152's Mention rule, once per Message, separately from the ordinary count (`0 <= mention_unread_count <= unread_count`). It is not a second read cursor and cannot expose an inaccessible Message. The target schema includes this field; before recognized Mentions can exist it is zero, never a placeholder zero once #152 persists Mentions. Mention parsing/target rules belong to #152. Its fixtures/count assertions stay proposed until that implementation exists.
- R3. #153 Search must call the same visibility/authorize function, including current caller revision and participant grant, before exposing a DM hit, snippet, author, facet or count. Owner/Admin and Mention targets gain no visibility by Role or Mention alone. Peer deactivation does not hide results from the still-authorized participant; stale caller access does. Search neither opens nor renews a DM. #152 resolves Mention candidates and unread results only within authorized resources; it cannot widen the Guest Member directory. These are shared permission fixtures for #151/#152/#153, not independent DM policies or copied API types.
- R4. Cursor browsing uses fixed 50-row pages, ordered by immutable DM `(created_at DESC, id DESC)` with UUID-value id as the tie-breaker. No cursor means first page; continuation selects keys strictly below the previous page's last key. `next_cursor` is null exactly when no further authorized row exists in that snapshot. It is opaque, integrity-protected and bound to Organization, caller Member, captured Membership revision and order version; malformed/tampered/wrong-scope cursors give 400 `invalid input` after caller authorization. It conveys no authority, hidden row count or server-held snapshot. Every request reauthorizes; losing access to an anchor does not authorize it or prevent browsing below its key. A stable authorized dataset traverses once without gaps/duplicates, including equal timestamps; later additions/access changes require a fresh first page or a new traversal. Peer rename/activity, sends and renewal never move an existing key.

  `channel_id` refresh returns only accessible requested ids in that same order, at most 50, with `next_cursor:null`; nonexistent, foreign, non-participant and lapsed-grant ids are indistinguishably omitted. It is not discovery by arbitrary id. Unknown queries, empty/duplicate/over-50 ids or cursor+ids give 400 `invalid input`. Across pages there is no shared MVCC snapshot or Organization-wide badge total. Each DM's Unread/Mention fields remain one R1/R2 baseline tuple; deduplicate overlapping pages/batches by Channel id and apply E6/P8 per access generation. Never sum duplicate rows, mix a count with another response's position, or treat an unloaded page as zero unread. Explicit recovery/browsing may traverse pages; periodic reconciliation never walks all cursors (E5).

### New send and canonical retry (B3)

- B3.1. Preserve Account Session -> input validation -> Organization/caller Channel authorization precedence. Then check the canonical `(organization_id,sender,client_message_id)` record **before peer eligibility**, send-limit charging or admission. An identical already-committed request returns the original Message with 201 even after peer deactivation or a lapse of the peer's grant. Changed body/Channel/Topic intent/reply target retains the existing 409 conflict. The sender's own current access is always required: deactivated, stale-revision, lapsed-grant and non-participant retries return their ordinary authorization error, never the canonical body.
- B3.2. Only a new send checks and retains peer eligibility through COMMIT: the peer must be active and have a grant capturing their current revision. Inactive peer gives 409 `dm_peer_deactivated`. An active reactivated peer whose grant is still lapsed gives 409 `dm_peer_access_lapsed`: explicitly open the existing DM with `renew:true` to renew access before sending; a default resolution-only open does not renew the peer. Never renew implicitly on send. This code was approved on 2026-11-14; it completes the current-revision requirement without widening B2's renewal authority. Both failures consume no seq, Message, read-position change or publication.
- B3.3. After a canonical miss, reject non-null DM Topic intent before charging/admission; a committed changed-intent retry has already returned its canonical conflict. New sends then keep S6's limiter -> admission order; the definitive peer check is inside the send transaction, so 429/503 may precede a peer error (as for transactional parent lookup). Paid tokens follow S6's existing no-refund rule. Before any persistence, reauthorize the captured sender revision/grant and check canonical retry again, then peer eligibility, then Reply parent, then allocation/insert. An in-flight identical duplicate waits for its execution and returns its committed canonical result after a fresh sender authorization; it never turns a committed send into `dm_peer_deactivated`. Different intents retain S5-S6 conflict/charging rules. A unique-key loser rolls back allocations and reads the canonical winner after reauthorization, without applying a new-send peer check.

## Deactivation and reactivation

- L1. Deactivation increments the participant's Membership revision and invalidates their DM grants/positions as it does private access. - L2. The active participant retains their current grant and read position: list, history, Reply-chain reads, Search, mark-read, subscribe/replay and already committed history remain available. - L3. After explicit B2 renewal, the same Channel/history is used with fresh grant lifetimes for the lapsed participants. A fresh subscribe/reselection is required. A Guest cannot initiate this renewal but can receive it from the other non-Guest participant. If both participants are active Guests and both grants are lapsed, neither can renew while a Guest; a permitted Organization Role change to non-Guest must precede explicit open. No automatic restoration or third-party Admin open is introduced.

## B4 durable discovery (Option A adopted, 2026-11-14)

**Guarantee.** Automatically process every currently accessible DM, including empty/old DMs and later gains/new Messages, despite lost notices or COMMITs delayed beyond recovery, without explicit browsing or a subsequent mutation. Access must remain usable until a generation-current authorized response; lost transient access cannot be disclosed. Discovery means processing an authorized identity before retention eviction, not simultaneous retention of every DM or an Unread baseline. The 200-row cap still applies. Requests must succeed under usable authority; outages, backoff and continuously invalidated responses cannot count as accepted progress.

**Choice.** Option A is a durable per-Member/revision discovery index, coalesced by DM rather than append-per-event. Option B (descending last-activity list/sweeps alone) is rejected: a moving row can jump above a saved descending boundary. Adopt independent rolling delta and immutable-key coverage lanes, each with one reserved O5 slot. Delta uses a fresh snapshot per request; coverage freezes its birth ceiling. Neither waits for the other. This bounds pending rows and prevents hot-row starvation without event history, per-client server snapshots, another poller or unbounded client state.

### Protocol and amendment of E4-E6

- P-D1 (durability/ownership). Channels owns the index/counter and SQL, exposed to Messages through a narrow transaction operation; Auth supplies scoped authority, preserving the module DAG. Unique key: (organization_id, member_id, membership_revision, channel_id). A row stores internal grant lifetime, immutable birth_position and mutable latest_position, never Message body/name/count. In the **same transaction** as create/renew/new send, upsert each currently authorized participant's row (including the sender); allocate a new position and move latest_position strictly forward, never backward. On first access in a revision set both positions; subsequent bumps preserve birth_position. Peer renewal bumps the still-current participant too; empty new DMs insert rows. No write for canonical retry, refused/rolled-back mutation or no-op open. Mark-read/peer identity alone use held-id refresh. A current grant's row is retained for zero-cursor bootstrap; no current-revision TTL or deletion after a client consumes it. One row coalesces any number of events, so pending distinct DMs per Member are ≤D≤N−1, independent of Message volume (N includes retained Member identities).
- P-D2 (commit order). Use a transactional counter-row UPDATE held through COMMIT per (Organization, Member, revision), never a sequence, clock or max(id). Every relevant path uses Organization -> existing Channel -> both Members (ascending id) -> existing grants (ascending Member id) -> both discovery counters (ascending Member id) -> index rows -> #142 Unread serialization/shared or exclusive #135 -> Message seq allocation, skipping unneeded stages but never acquiring an earlier stage later. Create ensures/locks counters before inserting grants/triggering baselines; renewal locks existing grants then counters/index before changing grants. Send takes counters/index before its existing exclusive #135/seq stage. Mark-read takes no discovery lock and keeps its existing released-watermark-before-Member/position order. Counter/index writes, grants/baselines and Message persistence are atomic; failure rolls back the mutation. Keep existing lock modes, authority held through COMMIT and no-lock post-COMMIT recipient reads. Implementation must prove this combined order against triggers on every path, including reciprocal sends and renewal.

  **Commit proof.** If T1 allocates p then pauses COMMIT, T2 for that Member cannot allocate q>p until T1 commits or rolls back; locking both counters in Member order prevents reciprocal allocation deadlocks. A read-only R1 snapshot before T1's COMMIT sees the old counter and old index row together, so its H<p, never an acknowledged hole. After COMMIT, both counter and moved/inserted row are visible; p exceeds that earlier H. If T2 commits, every earlier counter allocation has resolved. Rollback removes both bump and increment (the position may be reused safely); no phantom change becomes visible. Lost COMMIT replies, process restart with a surviving DB backend and later rollback obey the same serialization. Readers never wait on these counters or claim an early snapshot resolved an uncertain outcome; O5 keeps running. This orders discovery positions, independently of Message seq/#135's unchanged history barrier.
- P-D3 (REST and independent discovery lanes). Add GET /dms/changes with required lane=delta|coverage and optional opaque cursor, no client position/limit.   **Rolling delta lane:** no cursor starts at W=0. Every request takes a fresh committed counter H and selects W < latest_position ≤H in ascending latest_position order. If lookahead finds more, advance W to the last returned position; otherwise advance W to H and report caught_up. Only an accepted response advances W. No H/window is carried to the next request, so no slot is wasted finishing an obsolete frozen window. Coverage never changes W; delta never waits for coverage or changes its boundary. Repeated bumps coalesce to one candidate per DM per request; a later bump remains pending above W.

  One accepted page per lane per successful run gives quiet catch-up ≤Q runs, bootstrap ≤P runs and continuous-write identity progress ≤2P runs (≤2P pages of the coverage lane, ≤4P total discovery attempts). Under the recovery fixture's ≤33 s start spacing and ≤2 s final completion, time bounds are 33Q+2 s, 33P+2 s and 66P+2 s. If at most 500 distinct DMs are pending after the saved delta cursor and no new changes occur, all are processed within <=10 successful O5 runs (<=332 s), regardless of coverage progress. Above 500 pending, the 10-run deadline is not guaranteed: quiet 501 / 9,999 pending need ≤11 / 200 runs (≤365 / 6,602 s). For D=500 / 501 / 9,999, continuous-write progress is ≤20 / 22 / 400 runs (≤662 / 728 / 13,202 s). Faults/invalidations/backoff add attempts/runs without increasing the pending-set bound; they never permit stale acceptance. Delayed COMMIT enters these bounds when visible; an early accepted response cannot advance past it (P-D2/P-D3).

  Logical storage is one row per retained DM recipient/revision plus one counter per Member/revision, O(DMs), independent of event count; no server queue per socket or per-client history. Old revisions are reclaimable as soon as permanently invalidated, in bounded batches; measure their reclamation lag and physical dead-tuple/index bloat under upserts/autovacuum (logical boundedness alone does not prove disk boundedness). Cursor state is memory-only. Deployment restore still ends all Sessions and reconciles permissions before reopening; new sign-in discards all cursors/baselines and bootstraps restored rows. The restored counter may be above or below an old cursor, so numerical comparison is no restore detector. Missing post-backup data remains existing RPO data loss, not discoverable history; C8/A13 is not expanded.

## Decided (maintainer, 2026-11-14)

- B2: default open resolves an existing accessible DM before peer eligibility, without renewal; renew:true requests renewal. Reason: a lost response must not turn a resolution retry into a mutation.
- B2: an eligible caller without current access uses create/renew; renewal atomically replaces every lapsed participant grant and preserves current grants. Reason: reactivation alone restores no access or Unread lifetime.
- B3: new sends return 409 dm_peer_deactivated or 409 dm_peer_access_lapsed; authorized committed Message retries resolve first. Reason: sends never renew access or undo a committed canonical result.
- B4: Option A durable discovery is adopted; Option B is rejected. Every still-accessible DM must be processed despite lost notices/delayed COMMITs. Reason: first-page recovery can miss old or burst-created DMs.
- List/O5: 50-row creation-ordered pages, ≤200 retained rows and ≤5 total HTTP attempts/run are adopted. Reason: bound client work; continuations and retries share the ceiling, with fair carried progress.
- Discovery: coalesce by Organization/Member/revision/DM, with forward latest positions and immutable birth keys. Reason: O(DMs) pending/storage rather than O(events); finite coverage passes also prevent hot-row starvation.
- Discovery/O5: reserve independent rolling-delta and frozen-birth coverage slots; ≤500 distinct pending after saved delta cursor, then no changes, drain in ≤10 runs/≤332 s. Reason: coverage cannot delay catch-up; immutable keys prevent hot-row starvation. Three slots serve other lanes fairly.
- Bootstrap: zero coverage cursor enumerates current DM rows by birth position; delta starts independently at zero and browsing/recovery keep separate creation keys. Reason: old DMs remain discoverable without replay proportional to past events.
- Performance acceptance is adopted: 10,000 connections, 2,000 offered sends/s and all latency/DB/pool/memory/client budgets below the acceptance heading remain binding. Reason: verify the combined product target before implementation acceptance.
- Safety/ops: preserve permission, lock/COMMIT, Unread and canonical-retry rules; test them and measure index growth, WAL/lock load and backup/restore. Reason: bounded logical rows do not prove bounded physical storage or acceptable write cost.
