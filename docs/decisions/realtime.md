# Decisions: realtime

Real-time delivery, admission, queues and timeouts. Writing rules and the other parts: [index](../decisions.md).

## Real-time delivery

- #152 Mention counts inherit P8 and #307/#151 REST reconciliation (M4). REST is the authority for unwatched activity and cross-device marks. Notices never adjust counts directly, and there is no Mention event or timer.
- #195: SIGTERM/SIGINT stop acceptance and reject new requests, then signal concurrent socket closes (1001) on one shared watch and drain HTTP, detached sends/notifications and sockets before pool close. Count work before spawn/upgrade handoff, including retaining the upgrade callback drain guard, so caller disconnect and late scheduling cannot race pool close. Cap each Close at 1 s so one peer cannot delay the fleet.
- #195: retain provisional 60 s drain / 75 s Compose grace ([measured drains](../measurements.md#graceful-shutdown-and-reconnect-195)). Pre-admission SQL can outlast the drain deadline. Normal runtime teardown is outside the drain and can outlast it. Expiry skips teardown. Preserve normal teardown for now.
- #195/#18 crash, SIGKILL or drain expiry leaves COMMIT and Close uncertain. Recover by same-key retry and gap-free resubscribe (#135), clamping a future cursor to the committed high-water seq for restore compatibility (spec C8 requires generation rotation).
- #18/#116 one FIFO per connection (256 events and 256 subscriptions): register live, replay committed positions in 50-event pages, then flush all earlier queued events across Channels before `subscribed`. Preserve the silence barrier or replace its proof. No cross-Channel ordering guarantee. Overflow closes 4008 so each Channel replays from its own cursor.
- #18/#57 Messages owns post-COMMIT fan-out, using Auth's active-Member and Channels' visibility/grant predicates. Fresh non-locking checks on REST, subscribe, replay and fan-out see committed changes without cache/carryover. Set-bounded candidate ids and one batched query/shared payload avoid per-recipient SQL. Public access costs one boundary query. Authorization-query failure closes 4008 for replay.
- #57 PR B/#18 HTTP awaits persistence only. Persistence and sign-out survive cancellation to finish signaling. Arm detached post-COMMIT notification before COMMIT. Incomplete publication signals 1011 only to that Organization/Channel for replay. No durable queue.
- #179 chooses bounded cursor wait reservations over coalescing for hot-Channel admission/recovery and cross-Channel retention. Reserve before spawn (40 tasks/Organization/Channel, 2,048/Delivery). Take one of 40 global execution permits only after cursor lock. Saturation latches 1011. HTTP never waits. The 10 s deadline includes cursor wait and Drop releases reservations. Multi-Organization exhaustion and shared execution contention remain (#190). Cross-Organization fairness is unspecified.
- #190 uses 512 reservations/Organization over 256: both preserved the other Organization in the controlled probe, and 512 doubles burst headroom while leaving 1,536 slots for others. Acquire/remove under one short mutex, holding the global permit through removal to bound Organization entries to 2,048 and avoid stale-entry races, with no async wait or publication lock.
- #135/#148 separate gapless counter allocation from the committed-prefix proof. Take scoped exclusive transaction advisory lock before COMMIT. Readers take shared, then read in a separate READ COMMITTED statement. Absence in a lookup cannot prove rollback, nor can socket abandonment (#158). The extra write statement enables recovery with current authorization, canonical same-key retry and replay without another send. Watermark work including pool acquisition is capped at 10 s. Unresolved subscribe closes 1011.
- #135 only definite COMMIT rollback suppresses 1011: SQLSTATE class 23 or 40000/40001/40002/40P01. I/O, shutdown, 40003 and timeouts including 25P04 remain uncertain.
- #147 applies lock timeout with a materialized CTE before the shared lock to save a round trip. Keep counter SELECT separate for the fresh #135 snapshot.
- #57 item 4/#173 snapshot recipients under the Channel mutex. Global registry hands out counted leases. One cursor refills once for all recipients, reauthorizing each page. Cap shared refill at one 50-event page within queue capacity. Larger gap latches Channel 1011 and advances cursor. Authorization-query failure overflows that snapshot (4008) and advances. Page-read failure latches 1011 and keeps cursor. Old registrations retain their failed epoch. Later ones register live before the barrier-protected watermark. Both failures recover through replay, which stays paginated without this cap (S2). #178 owns item 6.
- #136 latches one watch per Organization/Channel epoch to avoid recipient iteration, locks and inline wakeups on HTTP failure (one lookup/swap and at most one spawn). A per-socket abort-on-drop monitor sends a oneshot to the biased loop (4001/4003 > 4008 > 1011). Outside Tokio keep the latch and skip wake. Idle CPU fell. Slower same-runtime bulk close reflected mass wake scheduling.
- #57 item 5 uses one loop interval (no task/env knob): Ping every 30 s, first Ping after last receive starts 60 s grace, so about 90 s idle to 1001. Keeps 10k sockets exercised with modest traffic. Writes share a 5 s deadline (4008 timeout, 1001 error). Answer peer Close within 1 s.
- #148 fixtures for behaviour not implemented require `proposed` and an owner Issue. Validate, skip and list them. Planned vocabulary elsewhere fails. Membership checks run at controller layer because the pure protocol has no I/O.
- #307 automatic socket registration with `activity_ready` gives a testable handoff outside control/subscription limits and their failure modes. ID/position-only invalidations keep REST the sole lists/counts authority and cover future Mention counts.
- #307 separates Organization-level revoke from subscription loss for immediate badge removal on unwatched Channels. Fresh identity/access proof limits notices (A3/A4), except proven own loss. A Guest's own directory must invalidate even without #294 or another Member mutation when its last shared Channel disappears.
- #307 O5 chooses periodic held-target reconciliation over durable publication/per-mutation outcome fences. A Channel-list snapshot lacks #135's barrier. Early success can precede delayed COMMIT. Coalesce up to 256 notice keys in a separate buffer and reset only with proven recipients because timing leaks even with an empty payload. O5 handles overflow/lost work without reconnect storms.
- #307 query, enqueue and first-handoff provenance fences stop paused publishers after loss/re-grant. Buffer purge or access alone cannot suffice. One conservative epoch and up to 256 retained lifetimes checked by batched fallback avoid per-visible-Channel tracking.
- #396/#422 tests join direct publishers and drain counted Activity work plus every permission transition before nonce Pong. Gate release, HTTP completion and Message Delivery drain are insufficient. Pong orders only ready work. Prove an authorized control in each phase to distinguish completion from rejecting stale work.
- #396/#543 retire epochs before recheck and publish, idempotently, so recheck cannot fence its own fresh query. Deactivation follows the same order even though its target-only recheck fences no other recipient.
- #396/#551 revocations carry mutation-lock-ordered captures through first handoff taken before mutation so late post-COMMIT retirement preserves newer Channel/Member work. Whole-set capture uses one sequence per registration and keeps sparse Channel changes only for live older captures, avoiding tracking every visible Channel per socket.
- #551 fallback checks live revocation identity/access through first handoff even with a missed hook. Restored access retires Channel work and Membership loss retires Member work.
- [#581](https://github.com/tkakkie/fukulow/issues/581) older private revocations need fresh proof of same Membership and absent access in current Role generation, including observation before post-COMMIT retirement. Restored access fences the original Channel sequence even if a reader in an older generation observes it. Effective Role change implies public access on at least one side and ends older public revocations despite subsequent loss.
- [PR #585](https://github.com/tkakkie/fukulow/pull/585) initial handoff rechecks run inline to authorize at first write poll. Shared five-second deadline can delay signals, receives and heartbeat for that socket. Advancing Role/proof revision permits another query within it. Timeout or unchanged proof ends the initial attempt.
- [#582](https://github.com/tkakkie/fukulow/issues/582) A recheck DB error invalidates prior proof and holds the original capture. Handoff holds its frame. Fallback holds snapshotted revocations and retires ordinary notices on failure. If fallback admits a hold during initial handoff, even a timed-out handoff parks its frame. The socket schedules retries without a task. Only the scheduled retry can authorize delivery, using a fresh identity and Channel access query capped at five seconds and the first-poll fences. Fallback can still fence a hold. Keep lock order entries → buffer → Tracking. Registration drop releases all holds.
- [#582](https://github.com/tkakkie/fukulow/issues/582) Reserve at most 64 holds per registration and 4,096 per process. Admission reclaims expired holds from any socket, including during replay. The shared deadline index is unlocked before acquiring another buffer. Overflow retires the incoming capture. A hold expires at 30 s or after five unsuccessful retries, with delays of 250 ms, 500 ms, 1 s, 2 s and 4 s. Budget 2 KiB per hold (including frame, capture and index entries) × 4,096 = 8 MiB, plus fixed per-registration bookkeeping. #746 measured 2,320,672 live heap bytes for 4,096 parked holds (567 B per hold; dhat test `measure_revocation_hold_heap`). The limits bound outage memory and retry work while preserving a short recovery window.
- [#582](https://github.com/tkakkie/fukulow/issues/582) In the periodic metrics event, `revocation_held` counts admissions, `revocation_delivered_after_hold` counts first write polls of `channel_access_revoked` and `revocation_recheck_errors` counts failed DB queries. `revocation_retired_registration_cap`, `revocation_retired_global_cap`, `revocation_retired_timeout`, `revocation_retired_retries` and `revocation_retired_fenced` count terminal drops. Fenced includes restored access and registration close. A written overflow reset releases the hold. Counters are process-local without identifiers.
- #307 freezes batch seq before authorization so later commits take a fresh snapshot. Preserve each recipient's other-sender activity. Exclude all sender sessions because own Messages do not affect counts. Revisit if lists sort by latest activity.
- #307 holds independent coverage per target. Summary position cannot prove aggregate freshness. All held REST targets share one refetch controller to apply identical retry, debounce and generation fences without adding dependencies. One in-flight request and one queued follow-up per target, with debounce and reset jitter, bound bursts to one fetch per target per debounce plus round trip. Transient failures keep targets dirty with capped backoff and fenced timers, recovering delayed notices without new mutations. Coalescing cannot bypass backoff. Post-invalidation snapshots and local access generations prevent stale additions/counts and directory exposure without server grant ids. Auth exposes mutation hooks implemented by Channels to preserve the module DAG.
- [#1084](https://github.com/tkakkie/fukulow/issues/1084) A9/A13 reuse a current main read ACK with both aggregate counts proven locally. Echo before ACK uses the existing debounce to preserve A10. A11 list authority and O5 cadence are independent.
- #151 dm_list_changed targets only the affected Member's live sessions with current Membership revision and DM access. Distinct REST target discovers even empty DMs. DM activity and read positions reuse ordinary notices (B4).
- #151 DM discovery reuses A5–A8 buffer/provenance and A9–A14/O5 so lost notices and delayed commits converge without unproven resets or a DM poller.
- #151 DM list responses use independent invalidation/access generations to fence old responses and publishers. Renewal replaces only lapsed lifetimes and unwatched recovery replaces baselines, preventing stale access/Unread revival after reactivation.
- #151 DM list base limits for paging, retention and [O5 allocation](../spec/dms.md#protocol-and-amendment-of-e4-e6) (R4/E5/P-D5) bound work without starving lanes or coupling their continuations.
- #151 B4 adopts durable discovery (A) over first-page or descending mutable-key recovery (B): no still-accessible DM can be lost after missed notices/delayed COMMIT. [P-D1–P-D7](../spec/dms.md#protocol-and-amendment-of-e4-e6).
- #151 coalesces discovery by Organization/Member/revision/DM. With no new changes, up to 500 DMs pending after the saved delta cursor drain within 10 successful O5 runs and 332 s under the [P-D7 fixture timing](../spec/dms.md#protocol-and-amendment-of-e4-e6), regardless of coverage progress.
- #159 S2 preserves delivery correctness without a recovery deadline: DB waits, scheduler, replay bounds and slow subscribers make latency a measurement target.
- #151 discovery rows coalesce per DM with advancing latest position and immutable birth key, bounding storage by DMs and preventing hot-row starvation.
- #151 bootstrap: a zero coverage cursor enumerates DM births. Delta starts independently at zero and browse/recovery retain separate creation keys, avoiding work proportional to past events.

## Admission and timeouts

- #196 B app pool defaults to `FUKULOW_DB_MAX_CONNECTIONS=20` (3..=100) and `FUKULOW_DB_ACQUIRE_TIMEOUT_MS=5000` (1..=5000 ms). Invalid settings fail. The default 5000 ms stays below the 10 s work deadline. Cap 100 bounds the SQLx queue and per-connection memory and fits ordinary PostgreSQL limits. The 10k socket target shares DB slots. Budget PostgreSQL capacity for operator connections. #388's 1000 ms caused PoolTimedOut/500 for unadmitted Channel-list/sign-in/session/other-Organization reads under overload where the base succeeded.
- #196 B keeps #156/#158's budget B=min(16, P−ceil(P/5)): ceil(B/2) sends, floor(B/2) watermarks, no borrowing, at most 1+4 per Organization/Channel. Exactly one Delivery per app pool, shared by its clones, sizes admission once and owns watermark access outside Messages. P=3 gives 1+1 and one spare, P=20 gives 8+8 and four spare, and larger pools still admit 8+8, P=1–2 is rejected because it leaves no watermark slot. Cap 5 failed startup bursts. 16 preserves at least 20% headroom (#147).
- #196 B headroom is shared across Session/auth/history/replay/notification/other reads. Unadmitted floods may starve Session checks, whose 5 s deadline closes 1011 rather than 4001/4003. FIFO can delay other Organizations. One pool and FIFO admission are shared, with no per-Organization DB reservation or fairness guarantee. #190 and #199 bound only their own resources. Four watermarks/Channel bound hot reconnects (~1,000/s at 4 ms). Clients back off.
- #196 B sets session-level statement_timeout=10s and jit=off, bounding ordinary queries/locks. The 8 s transaction timeout stays below 10 s statement/lock limits. 5 s acquire plus 8 s transaction wait can exhaust 10 s app work budget with unknown outcome (#135).
- #196 B startup parameters replace after_connect round trips without role settings, preserving db-roles.sql and frozen #153 manifest/base arm.
- #196 B binds -> checks Session settings -> serves to preserve #426's reviewed baseline patch. Failure exits without serving. TCP listen alone cannot indicate readiness.
- #196 B leaves RLS Account/Organization state transaction-local. Session statement timeout survives COMMIT/ROLLBACK and cleanup keeps local 1 s. Statement/COMMIT timeout is uncertain (#135).
- #196 B migrations disable statement/transaction timeouts on the discarded connection for long DDL, retaining the 5 s lock timeout. Never return its settings to the pool, including failure/cancellation.
- #88/#194 B bounds one-shot migration lock waits, including SQLx advisory, to 5 s to avoid queued DDL stalls. Fail before listening with no automatic retry.
- #156/#158 bound admission outside pool (1 s watermark, 30 s send then 10 s work after Session and Channel authorization). A queued send holds no connection. Short-wait locks still hold a connection. Try-lock causes contention failures and reconnect churn. Before counter allocation start exclusive barrier with transaction-local 10 s lock/8 s transaction timeout so DB can clear stuck work. No prepared transactions. Pool acquisition/initial canonical lookup precede barrier. The 40 s admission/work budget excludes pre-admission work and backend cleanup. Up to 8 s of DB work may outlive abandonment.
- #158 holds Channel/budget permits through explicit COMMIT/ROLLBACK or drop. On cancellation, deadline or error, detach/drop the connection rather than wait behind stuck COMMIT.
- #161/#178 reserve 256 send waiters per Organization/Channel and 2,048 overall before spawn. One Organization can fill the shared queue. Smaller caps raised refusals, larger ones roughly doubled accepted/delivery latency without capacity benefit. RSS did not decide.
- #161: queue-cap rejection and 30 s expiry return 503 ErrorBody and random 1–5 s Retry-After for shared saturation (not 429). DB error, unknown COMMIT and 10 s work expiry are 500/unknown.
- A dropped connection or response timeout leaves persistence uncertain. Detached work may still run for 30 s + 10 s. Retry with the same key (#158/#161).
- #198 B: admission sites only increment fixed categories. Report nonzero cumulative counts every 60 s with no entity labels. Gauge established WebSocket callbacks through Drop. Count attempted closes by fixed code, regardless of receipt.
- [#199](https://github.com/tkakkie/fukulow/issues/199) retains four Channel watermarks. Cap two lost ~31% throughput and raised recovery admission closes ~45% without significant 10k recovery benefit. Two stuck Channels can use all eight slots and cause other-Organization subscribe 1011. #213 defers fairness until real cross-tenant impact.
- #212 follows up handshake admission, listen backlog and accept pacing. #199 local recovery was handshake-bound and proves no production 10k capacity. #195 owns jitter.
- #196 A caps 32 sockets/Account across Organizations/Sessions and 12,000 globally (10k +20%). Account cap and budget checked first under one lock, without occupying global slot on refusal. Before any control DB work charge text/binary frames to socket burst 300/refill 4 per second and Account burst 600/refill 8 per second, sized for one/two 256-Channel bootstraps. Reconnect preserves Account budget. Exhaustion closes 4029 and clients back off as after 1011. Socket-cap and empty Account-budget refusals at upgrade return 503 socket_limit with random 1–5 s Retry-After to avoid upgrade storms, with one auth query still required.
- #196 A charges duplicate subscribe and keeps cursor to prevent a replay/query-cap bypass. Use unsubscribe+subscribe (two charges) or REST. See S4 for explicit epoch validation.
- #196 A retains at most 100,000 Account budgets. At cap drop idle full first, then evict the idle closest to full (restarts full), so depleted entries cannot block new Accounts. Active entries bounded by global socket cap.
- #196 A selects 8 KiB read buffers: 128 KiB costs ~1.15 GiB more RSS at 10k idle sockets. 4 KiB saves ~4 KiB more per socket but 8 KiB keeps a 16 KiB message to two reads. Idle CPU did not differ.
